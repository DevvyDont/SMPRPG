# Power Rating Rebalance

Status: **Design, not scheduled.** Supersedes the stat model recorded in `tools/statcalculator/REBALANCE_TARGETS.md` once implemented.

## 1. Summary

Replace the current "level" estimate (an unweighted average of skill levels and gear power) with a **Power Rating**: a single additive integer that every player, mob, and piece of gear carries on the same scale. Rating directly multiplies damage dealt, mismatched ratings apply damage penalties, and the underlying stat curve is flattened so that a level step costs a predictable, linear amount of effort instead of compounding.

Goals, in priority order:

1. One number that tells a player whether they belong in a fight, and tells a designer how hard content is.
2. Non-combat progression (farming, mining, fishing, woodcutting) contributes to combat potential directly, without being mandatory.
3. Content can be released "slightly out of reach" (raids, hazard zones) and remain tense rather than impossible, with drops closing the gap over a few runs.
4. Endgame numbers stay readable: ~100k mob EHP and ~5k player HP at level 100 instead of ~1M and six-figure hits.
5. Every number is derived from a small set of named constants and can be regenerated by the calculator tool.

Non-goals: changing skill XP curves, changing the loot/drop system beyond what rating requires, reworking abilities.

## 2. Problems with the current model

- `LeveledPlayer.getLevel()` is a mean of 7 skills + 6 equipment slots with the divisor floored at 11. Grinding a skill 0 to 50 moves the number by ~4. Holding a tool or an empty offhand changes it. It is truncated to an int, so single upgrades often show no change.
- Level has no combat effect. The damage pipeline in `EntityDamageCalculatorService` is attack attribute, defense `100/(D+100)`, difficulty. Nothing compares levels.
- Stats scale by `EntityGlobals.STAT_SCALING_FACTOR = 1.0964782` per level: 2.5x every 10 levels, ~10,000x over 100 levels. A level 110 encounter is ~6x the effort of level 100 (2.5x HP times 2.5x damage taken), so "a bit out of reach" is not expressible.
- Rarity and content level compete. A level 20 legendary lost to a level 30 rare from easier content, so it was skipped. Items carry no notion of potency independent of their blueprint.
- Group play is throttled: a mob's invulnerability frames are global, so several players (or several mobs) share one damage window. Group DPS is not additive and swarms are not more dangerous than a single mob.

## 3. Rating model

### 3.1 Scale and constants

| Constant | Value | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| `RATING_PER_LEVEL` | 20 | One content level. Mob rating = level x 20. |
| `RATING_SOFT_CAP` | 2000 | Level 100. Max skills + full level-100 gear, no augments. |
| `SKILL_RATING_PER_LEVEL` | 1 | Per skill level, every skill. 7 skills x 100 = 700. |
| `GEAR_RATING_PER_LEVEL` | 13 | Sum of slot weights below. 13 x 100 = 1300. |
| Natural mob level cap | 120 | Rating 2400. Deliberate hazard content, above any gear. |

Skills are 35% of the soft cap and gear 65%. The intended skill average at content level L is L itself, which makes the budget identity exact: `7L + 13L = 20L`. (An earlier target of 25% for skills is not compatible with "1 point per skill level" and a 2000 cap; 35% is accepted.)

Augments (enchantments, reforges, and any future small bonuses) sit **above** the cap and are the only way past 2000:

| Source | Rating per item | Max across 5 slots |
|---|---|---|
| Reforge | 0 to 5 | 25 |
| Enchantments (sum, per item) | 0 to 10 | 50 |

A fully min-maxed player lands around 2075.

### 3.2 Player rating

```
rating = sum(skill levels)
       + sum(slot_weight(slot) * potency(item)) for helmet, chestplate, leggings, boots, weapon
       + sum(augment rating) for those same items
```

- Only the four armor slots and the main-hand **weapon** count. Tools, rods, offhand items, and charms do not. Non-combat progression enters only through skill levels, never through what is held.
- Rating is cached on the player wrapper and recomputed on equipment change, skill level-up, and join. It is not recomputed per damage event.
- Difficulty profile does not affect rating.

### 3.3 Gear rating and potency

Every attribute item declares two things instead of one `getPowerRating()`:

- `contentLevel` (1 to 120): the level of the content that drops or crafts it.
- `rarity`: existing `ItemRarity`.

From these, **potency** is derived: `potency = contentLevel + rarityOffset(rarity)`.

| Rarity | Offset (levels) |
|---|---|
| Common | 0 |
| Uncommon | +1 |
| Rare | +2 |
| Epic | +4 |
| Legendary | +6 |
| Mythic | +8 |
| Divine | +10 |
| Transcendent | +12 |
| Special, Artifice | explicit per item |

Rating per item is `potency x slot weight`:

| Slot | Weight | At potency 100 |
|---|---|---|
| Helmet | 2 | 200 |
| Chestplate | 3 | 300 |
| Leggings | 3 | 300 |
| Boots | 2 | 200 |
| Weapon | 3 | 300 |

Potency is also the single input to the **stat budget** (section 5.3), so a level 20 legendary is, in every respect, a level 26 item. This is the mechanism that keeps a lower raid's legendary ahead of the next tier's rares, and it is a pure tuning decision: rarity means "punches above its content level by N levels".

Rules that make the ladder hold:

1. An item's content level is never higher than the level of the content it comes from.
2. Within one content level, acquisition difficulty and rarity must agree. A rare from a structure chest should not outrank a legendary from that structure's boss.
3. Each raid or dungeon tier's set should have an **upgrade path** into the next tier's set, consuming the previous piece plus materials from the new tier, so earlier content is never dead.
4. Double-dipping is intended: a weapon adds strength and rating; combat skill adds strength and rating; farming adds only rating.

Admin and gimmick items (Infinity, Hearty, etc., currently power 1000) declare explicit potency and are excluded from balance testing.

### 3.4 Mob rating

`rating = level x RATING_PER_LEVEL`. No per-mob work. Bosses, slayers, and raid encounters set their level as today. A level 110 raid reads as 2200.

## 4. Combat model

### 4.1 Expected EHP curve

Replace the exponential with a quadratic so each level step near the top is a small, predictable increase:

```
EHP(L) = BASE_EHP + EHP_QUADRATIC * L^2      BASE_EHP = 100, EHP_QUADRATIC = 10
```

| Level | EHP | vs L100 |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 110 | |
| 5 | 350 | |
| 10 | 1,100 | |
| 25 | 6,350 | |
| 50 | 25,100 | |
| 75 | 56,350 | |
| 100 | 100,100 | 1.00x |
| 110 | 121,100 | 1.21x |
| 120 | 144,100 | 1.44x |

Fallback candidate if the quadratic's early growth feels too steep in testing: exponential with factor `1000^(1/100) = 1.0715` (also 100k at L100, but 2.0x at L110). The calculator must support both so the choice is made on numbers, not taste.

### 4.2 Player defensive targets

Unchanged in shape from the June rebalance, rescaled to the new curve:

- Defense is the independent variable on the existing logistic S-curve, reaching **1900 at level 100** (95% resistance, `100/(1900+100) = 0.05`).
- HP is the top-off: `HP = EHP(L) / (1 + D/100)`, floored at 100. At level 100 that is **~5,000 HP**.
- Skill HP stays flat +2 per level across all skills. Armor carries ~85% of defense with 15% headroom for augments.

### 4.3 Hit budgets

| Constant | Old | New | Effect |
|---|---|---|---|
| `MOB_HITS_TO_KILL_PLAYER` | 9 | 18 (band 15 to 20) | On-level mob damage = EHP(L) / 18 |
| `PLAYER_HITS_TO_KILL_MOB` | 3.75 | 4 | Expected on-level player hit = EHP(L) / 4 |

Lower mob DPS is paid for by per-attacker invulnerability (4.6): one mob is forgiving, five are not.

### 4.4 Damage formula

```
dealt = weaponBase
      * (1 + strength / 100)
      * ratingMultiplier(attackerRating)
      * gapModifier(attackerRating, targetRating)
      * crit * enchantments * reforges * abilities
      * defenseMultiplier(targetDefense)
      * difficultyMultiplier
```

- `ratingMultiplier(r) = 1 + r / RATING_DAMAGE_DIVISOR`, divisor 1000. A 2000 player deals 3x a 0 player before any other factor. This is the knob that makes skill levels, reforges, and enchantments all give a small, visible damage increase through rating alone.
- `weaponBase` per potency is derived by the calculator so that an on-level player with intended strength and rating lands on the hit budget. It is an output, not an input.
- Order in the pipeline: rating and gap modifiers are applied in `EntityDamageCalculatorService` on `CustomEntityDamageByEntityEvent` at `HIGH` priority, after base and enchantment listeners and before the difficulty step, matching the existing comment convention in `ShamblingAbominationParent`.

### 4.5 Gap modifier

Gap is measured in levels: `gap = (targetRating - attackerRating) / RATING_PER_LEVEL`, positive when the attacker is under-rated.

| Constant | Value |
|---|---|
| `GAP_DEAD_ZONE_LEVELS` | 2 |
| `GAP_DEALT_PENALTY_PER_LEVEL` | 3% |
| `GAP_DEALT_FLOOR` | 10% |
| `GAP_TAKEN_PENALTY_PER_LEVEL` | 3% |
| `GAP_TAKEN_CAP` | +200% |

| Under by | Damage dealt | Damage taken |
|---|---|---|
| 0 to 2 | 100% | 100% |
| 5 | 91% | 109% |
| 10 | 76% | 124% |
| 20 | 46% | 154% |
| 30 | 16% | 184% |
| 35+ | 10% (floor) | 200% (cap) |

- Over-rated attackers get no bonus and no penalty; the flattened stat curve already carries that direction. Downscaling (level sync) for returning to old raids is a later option, listed in section 8.
- **PvP is exempt** from the gap modifier entirely.
- Gap applies to both directions of a mob/player interaction: the player's outgoing damage uses the dealt table, incoming mob damage uses the taken table.
- Rating-less sources (environment, fall, fire) are unaffected.

### 4.6 Per-attacker invulnerability frames

Replace the global `noDamageTicks` gate with a per-pair window:

- Track `(victim, attacker) -> last hit tick`. A hit from the same attacker inside the victim's window is cancelled; a hit from a different attacker is not.
- Window = `LeveledEntity.getInvincibilityTicks()` plus the existing armor-based bonus, same values as today.
- After applying a hit, reset the vanilla `noDamageTicks` so Minecraft's own global gate no longer blocks other attackers.
- Projectiles attribute to their shooter, as the damage service already does.

Consequences: a swarm's damage scales linearly with mob count ("over-aggro" is punished), and group DPS on a boss is additive, which raids require.

### 4.7 Difficulty profiles

Rating is unaffected by profile. With the hit band widened to 18, the incoming multipliers can relax without changing the relative feel:

| Profile | Incoming damage today | Proposed | Effective on-level hits to die |
|---|---|---|---|
| Wanderer (Easy) | 0.5x | 0.5x | 36 |
| Adventurer | 1.0x | 1.0x | 18 |
| Ascendant (Hard) | 2.0x | 1.5x | 12 |

Ascendant at 1.5x is still harder than today's Adventurer (9 hits). XP and drop-rate modifiers unchanged.

## 5. Content design

### 5.1 Dripfeed cadence as raid tiers

Server sections unlock over time (Nether after days, End after a week, raids later). Each unlock is a tier. The expected rating at unlock is the section's minimum level x 20, and the section's drops have content level equal to its level band.

### 5.2 Out-of-reach content

A raid is set 10 levels above the current gear ceiling (e.g. level 110, rating 2200, EHP 121k). A full-cap group enters at gap 10: deals 76%, takes 124%. The enrage and doom timers in `BossInstance` provide the DPS floor; mechanics and the taken-side penalty provide the coordination pressure.

Drops are potency 110 (content level 110, rarity offsets on top). Each armor piece closes 20 to 30 rating, so a group reaches parity in a handful of clears. Hazard zones (far-spawn level 120 mobs) are the same model with no loot cadence.

Raid support work:

- Boss max HP scales with participant count (currently fixed).
- Full-loot participation threshold moves from 20% of boss HP per player to a flat contribution floor, since 20% cannot be met by six or more players.

### 5.3 Procedural gear budget

A single `GearBudget` function turns `(slot, potency)` into clean stats and rating, so gear can be dropped at any level with no hand-tuning:

- Defense share: logistic curve value at `potency`, x 85%, split by existing piece weights (helmet 21, chest 36, legs 28, boots 15).
- HP top-off: `max(0, EHP(potency)/(1 + D/100) - skillHp(potency))` split by the same weights.
- Weapon base: `EHP(potency) / PLAYER_HITS_TO_KILL_MOB / (1 + intendedStrength(potency)/100) / ratingMultiplier(20 * potency)`.
- All outputs pass through a soft-rounding step (reuse `EntityGlobals.softRoundHealth`-style tiers) so lore shows round numbers.

Blueprints keep the ability to override individual stats for flavour, but the default is the budget.

### 5.4 Catch-up gear (escape hatch)

When a new section unlocks, the previous section's standard set becomes obtainable without that section's hard content: craftable from its common materials or sold by a vendor, at Rare potency for its level. This keeps late joiners able to reach the current tier and matches how WoW resets the floor each tier. Raid legendaries stay raid-only; the catch-up set is deliberately a step below them.

### 5.5 Display

- Nametags, chat prefix, action bar, XP bar option, and structure warnings show rating instead of level. Mob nametags show rating; colour the bracket by gap (green within dead zone, yellow to red as the gap grows).
- Item lore shows `Power +N` (slot rating) and potency source (content level and rarity).
- A `/power` or profile view breaks the total down: skills, each slot, augments.

## 6. Implementation plan

### Phase 0: Calculator and number validation (no plugin changes)

Rework `tools/statcalculator` to model everything above. Outputs are tables committed under `tools/statcalculator/`.

- New EHP curve (both candidates), rating budgets, gear budget function, gap tables.
- Simulations at levels 1, 5, 10, 25, 50, 75, 100, 110, 120: on-level time-to-kill both directions, player survivability vs 1, 3, and 5 mobs with per-attacker i-frames, under-rated raid at gap 5 / 10 / 15, and a skills-only vs gear-only player at each level.
- Verify the intended-skill-average assumption against `SkillGlobals` XP curves: at each content level, how many hours of each skill reach the intended average.
- Exit criteria: on-level hits land inside the bands; L110 raid at gap 10 is beatable by the simulated group inside the enrage timer; no level where a skills-only player out-damages a gear-only player.

### Phase 1: Rating model and display

- `PowerRating` value type and `PowerRatingService`: player rating cache with invalidation on equipment, skill, and join events; mob rating from level.
- `IAttributeItem.getPowerRating()` replaced by `contentLevel` + rarity-derived potency; blueprint migration (~150 files, mostly mechanical). `calculateValue` (sell price) switched to potency so prices do not scale by the square of the new numbers.
- Display changes from 5.5. No balance change yet; the old curve stays.

### Phase 2: Combat hooks, zero-effect

- Gap modifier listener with all constants at zero effect, toggled via config for testing.
- Per-attacker i-frames behind a config flag.
- PvP exemption, projectile attribution tests.

### Phase 3: Curve flattening and rebalance

- Swap `EntityGlobals` curve and hit constants; `EntityConfiguration.scale` follows automatically.
- Regenerate the 56 `CustomEntityType` HP/damage values and boss HP from the calculator (they are stored as absolutes and would otherwise inherit the wrong multiplier).
- Regenerate armor and weapon stats through `GearBudget`; review flavour overrides.
- Retune difficulty multipliers, enable gap and i-frame constants at their designed values.

### Phase 4: Raid support

Party-size HP scaling, participation threshold, potency drops, upgrade paths, catch-up vendor.

## 7. Testing plan

Testing the numbers is a deliverable, not a follow-up.

- **Unit tests** for every formula in sections 3 and 4: rating composition, potency, gap tables, rating multiplier, EHP curve, gear budget rounding. Table-driven against the calculator's committed outputs so the plugin and the tool cannot drift.
- **Calculator simulations** from Phase 0 re-run after every constant change; results diffed in review.
- **In-game playtest matrix** on a staging world, one session per row, with time-to-kill and deaths logged to a file by a debug command:
  - Fresh character at L1 and L10 with intended gear; solo vs 1 and vs 5 mobs.
  - L50 intended; L50 at skills-only; L50 at gear-only.
  - L100 cap vs L100 mob, vs L110 raid solo and in a group of 4, vs L120 hazard.
  - Ascendant and Wanderer at L10 and L100.
  - PvP at equal rating and at a 500-rating gap (expect no gap effect).
- **Regression checks**: existing boss fights still complete inside enrage timers; slayer tiers remain in order; structure warnings fire at the right gap.

## 8. Open questions

- Level sync (downscaling over-rated players) for returning to old raids: wanted for group play, deferred until raids exist.
- Whether accessories or charms should ever count toward rating. Current answer: no.
- Whether Special and Artifice rarities need a potency rule or stay explicit.
- Exact catch-up set per section and its vendor or currency.
- Whether mob rating should display as a range for procedurally levelled vanilla mobs (location variance is +-3 levels).

## 9. Risks

- This invalidates the June 2026 armor rebalance; every stat in `ItemArmor.getDefenseFromItemType` and the hardcoded sets is regenerated. Budget the time.
- Sell prices, combat XP (`level x multiplier x 2`), and coin drops key off level and must be checked after the curve changes.
- Per-attacker i-frames interact with knockback and the existing bow-stacking exploit guard; both need explicit tests.
- Players will optimise the number. Only armor and weapon count, and rating is cached, specifically to blunt this.
